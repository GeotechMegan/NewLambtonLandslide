New Lambton Landslide Evidence Project

This site is a shared, evolving evidence base. It exists to document what has occurred, preserve primary material, and support careful, informed decision-making.

Contributions are welcome from residents, researchers, and subject-matter experts. To keep the material credible, usable, and fair, please follow the guidelines below.

1. Purpose of contributions

  • Document what happened, when, and who knew what
  • Archive primary sources (documents, maps, records, correspondence)
  • Clearly distinguish evidence from interpretation
  • Support informed decisions by residents, agencies, experts, and legal advisers

This is not an advocacy or opinion forum.

2. Evidence first

  • Prioritise primary sources (official documents, maps, approvals, reports, photos, dated correspondence)
  • Clearly cite where information comes from
  • Where possible, upload or link the original document

It's OK to summarise from memory alone, but this needs to be clearly labelled as a recollection. This is a different type of evidence and needs to be distinguished as such.

3. Separate fact from interpretation

  • Fact: verifiable information supported by a source
  • Observation: what was seen or experienced (date, location, by whom)
  • Interpretation: analysis or inference drawn from facts (clearly marked)
  • Question: an unresolved issue requiring further investigation

4. Use standard status language

When updating task statuses, use only the approved terms listed on the Tasks page.

5. Neutral, precise language

  • Avoid emotive or accusatory wording
  • Do not assume intent or motive
  • Use precise references (e.g. “Council correspondence dated 12 June 2019 states…”)

6. Respect privacy and sensitivity

  • Do not upload personal or private information unless essential and appropriate
  • Avoid speculation about individuals
  • Flag sensitive material before publishing

7. Think in timelines and systems

Strong contributions place events in time, show how systems evolved, and connect decisions across departments and years.

8. Ask questions openly

If something is unclear, add it as a question rather than a conclusion. Unanswered questions are valuable.

9. Aim for longevity

Assume this material may be read in future by lawyers, engineers, government decision-makers, and journalists.

10. When in doubt

If you are unsure how to contribute, mark the material as “requires review” or contact the site curator. Care and accuracy matter more than speed.

Thank you. This project depends on careful, thoughtful contributions.
